# Time-Frequency Analysis: Application of Second-Order Time Rearrangement Synchronous Compression Transform in Draupner Wave Analysis [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: The objective of this paper is to develop effective and innovative methods for processing non-stationary multicomponent signals. Among the common methods, Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) [5] are the simplest linear transformations, widely applied in various applications such as audio [6], biomedical [7], seismic or radar.
Unfortunately, these tools are limited by the Heisenberg-Gabor uncertainty principle. Therefore, the representations obtained are ambiguous, have poor energy concentration, and need to balance between time or frequency localization accuracy. Another method, reassignment method [8], [9] was introduced as a mathematically elegant and effective solution to improve the readability of Time Frequency Representation (TFR). Inconveniently, reassignment provides an irreversible TFR, which limits its interest for analysis or modeling applications.
Recently, Synchronous Squeezing [10], [11] was introduced as a variant of reassignment technique because it can provide sharpened and reversible TFR. This reconstruction capability makes this method continuously attract attention because it paved the way for an unlimited number of applications based on synchronous compression, such as noise removal [12], signal component extraction or separation [10], [13], [14], [4].
Nowadays, efforts are being made to effectively compute several versions of synchronous compression of several linear transformations, such as STFT, CWT or S transform [15], [16], and use enhanced instantaneous frequency estimators [17], [18], [19] to improve the localization of emphasized signals. To deal with pulse and emphasized signals, a new synchronous compression method called Time Reassignment Synchronous Compression Method [20] was introduced. However, this method cannot effectively process mixed content signals containing pulse and periodic components.
In this paper, we propose a new transformation called second-order horizontally synchronized compression, aimed at improving the time redistribution of energy localization and readability while maintaining invertibility. To this end, we employ an enhanced group delay estimator that is mathematically related to our previous results [18].
Article illustrations
Conclusion introduces a new time redistribution synchronization compression, known as second-order horizontally synchronized compression, for short-time Fourier transform. Our experiments demonstrate a significant improvement in computing the time-frequency representations of invertible and sharpened pulse signals, which cannot be achieved with vertical synchronization compression. Furthermore, we have proven the efficiency of this technique when applied to synthetic and real-world signals. In fact, our method helps in discovering new signal components in Draupner wave signals, which may aid in better understanding of anomaly wave phenomena. Future work includes theoretical strengthening of this approach and development of new applications.
